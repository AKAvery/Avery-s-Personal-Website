The role

Through Convergent I took charge of a 4-person tech team to build a medical school admissions copilot for UT Austin premedical students. At the end of the semester we presented it to CEOs and investors at the Convergent showcase.

This was my first time being responsible for other people’s work as well as my own, and the lesson was mostly about interfaces: the architecture decisions that mattered were the ones that let four people work without blocking each other.

The product

Premed advising is a problem of scattered information. The data that determines whether an application is competitive exists, but it is spread across dozens of school pages, PDFs, and forum threads, and none of it is personalised. The copilot pulls it into one place and answers against the student’s own profile.

I architected a Next.js 14, TypeScript, and Supabase product spanning more than 20 routes:

  • AI advisor — a context-aware Claude Sonnet agent that personalises answers from the user’s GPA, MCAT, and activities profile.
  • Chance calculator — admissions odds against real school-by-school data.
  • Interview prep.
  • School explorer and compare.
  • Activity tracker.

Because the whole thing turns on students’ own academic records, access control was not an afterthought: every table sits behind Postgres row-level security, with Google OAuth for identity.

Data pipeline

None of the product works without trustworthy data underneath it, so I built a Python pipeline using pdfplumber, BeautifulSoup, and pandas that aggregates more than 30 admissions data sources into a single normalised schema. Getting 30 sources to agree on what a field means turned out to be most of the job.
